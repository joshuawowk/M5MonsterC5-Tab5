Build the MonsterC5 on a Proto Module

The build from the M5MonsterC5 Setup Guide, made real: an ESP32-C5 running JanOS with both an Ebyte E01-2G4M27SX (nRF24+PA/LNA) and an E07-433M20S (CC1101+PA) on one proto board, seated on the Tab5's M-Bus. Every wire is drawn where it goes — pin to pin, colour-coded, with the net list beside it.

Host linkM-Bus UART0 · 4 wires
Radio busC5 SPI2 · shared · select switch
RadiosE01 2.4 GHz + E07 433 MHz
Modules1× or 2× M5 Proto
i

The short version. Both radios share the C5's one SPI2 bus (SCK/MOSI/MISO). They also share the single chip-select G3 and control line G4 that stock JanOS drives — so the two can't both sit live on the bus at once. A DPDT slide switch hands G3+G4 to whichever radio you're running; you flash one radio app at a time anyway. Everything you need to buy and solder is below.

01

What you're building, and the three real constraints

Nothing about the firmware changes — this is the Setup Guide's wiring moved onto a proto board, with both radios present. Three things make it more than "drop in two modules," and the whole build is shaped around them.

constraint 1

One radio at a time

JanOS drives one chip-select (G3) and one control pin (G4) for the radio header. Tie both modules to them and they fight on MISO. The select switch connects only the radio you're using. You run the CC1101 app or the nRF24 app — never both — so nothing is lost.

constraint 2

The E07 has a PA switch

The E07-433M20S is a +PA part: pins TX_EN/RX_EN steer its RF switch. Stock firmware doesn't drive them. For a receive/scan build you hard-tie RX_EN→3V3, TX_EN→GND. To transmit you wire both to spare GPIO (Power & PA).

constraint 3

490 mA TX bursts

The E01 pulls ~490 mA at 27 dBm. Don't hang that off the C5's little regulator — give the radios their own 3V3 LDO off M-Bus 5V with a fat bulk cap. And both Ebyte parts are 1.27 mm SMD: they need adapter boards, not a 2.54 mm hole.

Two pin domains — keep them straight

host side

The M-Bus tap — 4 wires

All the proto board takes from the Tab5: 5V, GND, and the UART0 pair G37/G38 (ESP32-P4 pins). That is the entire interface to the tablet.

board side

The C5 build — its own GPIO

The radios, their power and select switch hang off the ESP32-C5's own pins (G6/G8/G5/G3/G4), wired across the proto grid — never onto M-Bus pins. The C5's console G11/G12 is the one net that bridges to the M-Bus tap.

03

Wiring — where every wire goes

Two harnesses. First the four wires to the Tab5. Then the radio bus: the C5's SPI2 fanned to both modules, with the select switch on the shared chip-select and control lines.

Harness A — the C5 to the Tab5 (4 wires)

Harness B — the radio bus (C5 to both modules)

The C5's SPI2 (SCK G6 / MOSI G8 / MISO G5) is a shared bus: every device hangs off the same three wires. Both radios tap it. What makes a device respond is its own chip-select. Stock JanOS gives the radio header exactly one — G3 — plus one control line G4 (the CC1101's GDO0 / the nRF24's CE). The select switch routes that pair to one module. The other module sits on the bus with its CSN pulled high: deselected, MISO tri-stated, silent.

i

Pad numbers follow the Ebyte drawings. E07-433M20S: 12 pads on one edge (1 GND … 12 GND) plus 13 ANT and 14–16 GND on the far edge. E01-2G4M27SX: 8 pads on one edge (1 VCC, 2 CE, 3 CSN, 4 SCK, 5 MOSI, 6 MISO, 7 IRQ, 8 GND) plus 9, 10 GND on the sides. Shield up, pad edge away from you, they count right-to-left. For a to-scale drawing with the castellations in their real positions, see the XIAO two-board build.

Want both live, no switch? Remap the firmware.

The switch exists because stock JanOS hard-codes CS=G3 and ctrl=G4 for both radios. Give each radio its own chip-select in the firmware and you can leave both wired permanently — no switch, though still one app at a time. Edit the two headers, pick free C5 pins, rebuild:

// JanOS/ESP32C5/components/cc1101/include/cc1101.h #define CC1101_PIN_CS 3 // keep G3 for the CC1101 #define CC1101_PIN_GDO0 4 // JanOS/ESP32C5/components/nrf24_jammer/nrf24_jammer.c #define NRF24_CS_PIN 1 // move nRF24 CSN to a free pin (e.g. G1 / J1-6) #define NRF24_CE_PIN 0 // move nRF24 CE to a free pin (e.g. G0 / J1-5)

Then skip the DPDT: wire E07 CSN→G3, E01 CSN→G1, E07 GDO0→G4, E01 CE→G0, everything else as the net list. Keep clear of the strapping pins (G2/G7/G25/G26/G27/G28) when you choose.

i

Already done for you on the XIAO. The XIAO C5 build ships a JanOS branch with exactly this dual-chip-select remap (plus the XIAO's own pad map), with both radios drawn wired at once and no switch. If you're on a XIAO — or just want the no-switch wiring worked end to end — start there.

05

Power & the PA switch

Both modules are +PA parts and both bite back if you skimp on the supply or feed them over 3.6 V. This is where builds that "almost work" fall down.

Give the radios their own 3V3

The E01 draws ~490 mA at 27 dBm in millisecond bursts (RX ~22 mA). The C5's on-board regulator won't hold 3V3 under that. Run an LDO ≥ 600 mA from M-Bus 5V, and put 470 µF + 0.1 µF right at each radio's VCC/GND. Star the grounds at the LDO.

3.3 V is the ceiling

Over 3.6 V permanently damages both modules. They are not 5 V-tolerant on any pin — power or signal. The C5 is a 3.3 V part, so its GPIO are fine; just never let the M-Bus 5V touch a module pin.

Driving the E07's RF switch (TX_EN / RX_EN)

The E07-433M20S puts a PA and an antenna switch in front of the CC1101. Two pins steer it, and they're mutually exclusive:

  • Receive / scan only (what stock firmware does): tie RX_EN → 3V3 and TX_EN → GND. The module sits in receive; the CC1101 talks over SPI as normal. No firmware change.
  • Transmit too: wire TX_EN and RX_EN to two spare C5 GPIO and drive TX_EN high (RX_EN low) only while sending, low again after. Never both high.
!

The bare-CC1101 firmware never touches TX_EN/RX_EN. If you leave them floating the PA switch is undefined and range collapses — always tie them, even for receive. The plain E07-433M10S (10-pin, no PA) has no such pins; this applies only to the M20S you ordered.

06

Flash & verify

1

Flash JanOS onto the C5 off-board

Socket the C5 so you can pull it. Build for the C5 target and flash over its USB/UART — radio remaps (XIAO, or the dual-CS edit) must be built in before it goes on the board.

# ESP32-C5 → IDF 6.1 source ~/.espressif/v6.1/esp-idf/export.sh idf.py -C JanOS/ESP32C5 set-target esp32c5 build flash
2

Seat the board & power from the Tab5

Plug into the M-Bus (or clip the four leads). M-Bus 5V (pin 28) is switchable — if the board is dark, enable the bus or power the C5 from USB to prove the build first.

3

Confirm the link on the Tab5

The Tab5 app talks UART0 at 115200 8N1 and expects JanOS 1.7.6+ (a mismatch warns, it doesn't block). A Monster appears when TX/RX are crossed and GND is common.

4

Pick a radio, then prove the bus

Flip the select switch to the radio you flashed the app for. Run the radio's self-test: a good SPI read-back means the shared bus and that radio's CSN/CE are right. Flip to the other radio only after stopping the first app.
