Build the MonsterC5 on a Proto Module

The build from the M5MonsterC5 Setup Guide — an ESP32-C5 running JanOS with a CC1101 or nRF24 radio — soldered onto an M5Stack Module Proto that seats on the Tab5's M-Bus. The diagrams below are drawn to scale from measured part dimensions, so the placements are real, not cartoons.

Proto boardM5Stack M001 · 54.2 × 54.2 mm
Grid2.54 mm · 260 holes
Host linkM-Bus UART0 · 4 nets
RadioCC1101 / nRF24 on C5 SPI2
!

Measure before you solder. Every footprint below is from a first-party dimension drawing or datasheet (sources at the foot of the page), and the diagrams are drawn to those numbers. Three things are not confirmed and must be checked on your bench:

• The M-Bus connector's pitch and exact position on the M001 — the 2.54 mm figure is the proto board's hole grid; the 2×15 M-Bus header is assumed to sit on that grid. • Whether the M001 mechanically mates the Tab5's rear M-Bus — the M001 is a Core-stack module; the Tab5 is a tablet. Confirm the connector seats before you rely on it. • The Ebyte modules' exact pad maps — their datasheet pad drawings are image-only; pad pitch (1.27 mm) and size are confirmed, individual pad positions are not.

01

Two pin domains

Nothing about the wiring changes from the Setup Guide — it just moves onto a proto board. Keep two sets of pins straight, because they belong to different chips.

host side

The M-Bus tap — 4 nets

All the proto board takes from the Tab5: 5V, GND, and the UART0 pair G37/G38 (ESP32-P4 pins). That is the whole interface to the tablet.

board side

The C5 build — on the grid

The radio, SD and GPS hang off the ESP32-C5's own GPIO (G6/G8/G5/G3/G4…), wired across the proto grid — never onto M-Bus pins.

The C5's console UART0 (G11 TX / G12 RX) is the one net that bridges the two: it crosses to the M-Bus tap's G37/G38. Everything else on the board stays on the C5.

02

Which C5 — and why size decides

The three board options

MonsterC5 / DevKitC-1

Stock JanOS runs unchanged — every radio/SD/GPS pin is on the header. But at 53.6 mm it fills the proto board; little grid left for the radio. Flash over the DevKitC's UART port.

XIAO ESP32-C5

21 mm, dual-band, $7. Fits with room for the radio — but needs a JanOS rebuild with remapped pins (see Wiring). Console G11/G12 already lands on pads D6/D7.

A bare C5 module

Smallest, but you add your own 3V3 regulator and USB-UART for flashing. Only worth it if you're laying a custom board.

i

Other ESP32s won't do the sub-GHz-less Wi-Fi job either way, but only the C5 is dual-band. A XIAO ESP32-C3/C6/S3 is 2.4 GHz only and is not the MonsterC5 target. If you have a mix of boards, the XIAO ESP32-C5 is the one that fits this build.

!

Your Ebyte modules are 1.27 mm SMD, not plug-ins. The E01 (18 × 14.5 mm) and E07 (18 × 32 mm) have 1.27 mm castellated/stamp pads and an IPEX RF jack — they don't drop into the 2.54 mm grid. Hand-wire fine leads (30 AWG) to a patch of grid, or mount them on a 1.27→2.54 breakout. And decouple the nRF24+PA/LNA: 100 nF + 10 µF + 100 µF at its pins, ideally its own 3V3 LDO off 5 V — see Decouple the nRF24.

If you use a XIAO ESP32-C5 — the pin remap

The XIAO exposes a different subset of GPIO than the DevKitC. Its console G11/G12 lands on pads D6/D7, so the M-Bus tap works as-is — but JanOS's stock radio/SD pins (G6/G5/G3/G4/G13/G14) are not on the XIAO's pads. For the Wi-Fi/BLE suite you need nothing more than the tap. To add the radio or SD you must rebuild JanOS with the pins remapped to pads the XIAO actually breaks out.

Change the defaults in components/cc1101/include/cc1101.h, components/nrf24_jammer/nrf24_jammer.c, and the SD/GPS defines, then rebuild for esp32c5. Console and Wi-Fi need no change.

The M-Bus pinout, in full

From the official M5Stack Tab5 page. The four tapped nets are marked. Leave EN (pin 6) and the undocumented HVIN nets alone.

✕

One power source, and keep clear of EN/HVIN. Never feed the C5 from the M-Bus 5V and its own USB at once. A stray short to EN (pin 6) reboots the Tab5; the HVIN nets (25/27/29) are undocumented — leave them open.

05

Flash, seat, verify

1

Flash the C5 first, off the board

Flash JanOS and prove it on a serial monitor before it goes on the proto board. On a DevKitC use the UART USB-C port (not native USB); on a XIAO, rebuild with the remapped pins first if you want the radio. Full flash flow and offsets are in the M5MonsterC5 Setup Guide.

2

Flash the Tab5 app

ESP32-P4 image — web flasher or esptool (bootloader 0x2000, partitions 0x8000, app 0x10000). Again, see the Setup Guide.

3

Seat on the M-Bus and boot

Mount the proto module, boot the Tab5. It probes M-Bus/Grove/USB, finds the C5 on UART0, and raises MONSTER DETECTED.

!

Version & one link. The Tab5 app wants JanOS 1.7.6 or newer (a mismatch is a warning, not a wall). Pick a single link — M-Bus or Grove or the UART USB port; all three land on the same UART0.

06

If something's off

Symptom → check
SymptomLikely cause / fix
Module won't fit the Tab5 backThe M001 is a Core-stack module — confirm it mates the Tab5's M-Bus before building. If not, tap the four nets with wires instead of seating the connector.
Board never powers upM-Bus 5V (pin 28) is switchable and may be off — power the C5 from its USB to confirm, or enable the bus.
Tab5 shows no MonsterTX/RX not crossed — C5 G11→M-Bus G38 (pin 13), C5 G12→M-Bus G37 (pin 14); confirm common GND; only one link wired.
XIAO: radio never detectedStock JanOS pins aren't on XIAO pads — you must rebuild with the remap table above. Wi-Fi works without it.
Radio dead / erratic3V3 only — >3.6 V permanently damages the E01/E07. Check CSN=G3 (10k↑), control=G4 (4.7k↓), and the PA/LNA decoupling.
Reboots when you touch the boardA short to M-Bus EN (pin 6) resets the P4. Keep wiring clear of pins 6 and the HVIN pins (25/27/29).
