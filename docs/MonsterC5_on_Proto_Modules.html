Build the MonsterC5 on a Proto Module

Take the same DIY MonsterC5 — an ESP32-C5 running JanOS, with a CC1101 or nRF24 radio, SD and GPS — and build it onto one or more M5Stack Proto Modules that seat on the Tab5's M-Bus. No dangling cable: the host link and power come straight off the connector, and the whole add-on rides on the back of the tablet.

01

The idea — and two pin domains

The shipping MonsterC5 is just an ESP32-C5 the Tab5 drives as a serial terminal. The DevKitC build proves that with one cable. Here we do the same wiring, but solder it onto an M5Stack Proto Module so it plugs into the Tab5's M-Bus instead. Keep two sets of pins straight — they are different chips.

host side

The M-Bus tap — 4 nets

Everything the build takes from the Tab5: 5V, GND, and the UART0 pair G37/G38 (P4 pins). That is the entire interface to the tablet.

board side

The C5 build — on the proto board

The radio header, SD and GPS all hang off the ESP32-C5's own GPIO (G6/G8/G5/G3/G4, etc.), wired on the Proto Module — never onto M-Bus pins.

02

What you need

core

ESP32-C5

An ESP32-C5-DevKitC-1 (N8R8) is the easy route — onboard 5V→3V3 LDO, USB for flashing, and the RGB LED JanOS uses. A bare C5 module works if you add your own regulator.

substrate

M5Stack Proto Module ×1–2

The perfboard that seats on the M-Bus. One module fits the C5 + radio; add a second for the SD/GPS and the PA/LNA power stage. Confirm it mechanically mates the Tab5's rear connector first.

radio

CC1101 or nRF24L01+PA/LNA

E07-433M20S (CC1101) for sub-GHz, or E01-2G4M27SX (nRF24+PA/LNA) for 2.4 GHz. They share the header — fit one. 3.3 V only.

optional

microSD + GPS

A 3.3 V SPI microSD breakout and an ATGM336H / M5 GPS for wardrive logging and location.

power

Decoupling + LDO

100 nF + 10 µF + 100 µF per radio; for the PA/LNA, an AMS1117-3.3 / AP2112K (≥500 mA) off 5 V so the radio doesn't share the C5's LDO.

glue

Headers, sockets, wire

A socket strip for the C5 and a 7-pin socket for the swappable radio, plus the M-Bus pass-through pins and hookup wire.

03

The M-Bus tap

Solder to exactly four M-Bus nets. The link is UART0 — cross TX↔RX: the C5's TX lands on the host's RX. The full connector is below for reference; the four tapped pins are marked.

Host TXpin 14 · TXD0 G37G12 (U0RXD)host transmit → board receive

!

The M-Bus 5V is switchable, and one power source only. Pin 28 is the EXT_5V_BUS, gated by EXT5V_EN — it may be off until the Tab5 enables it. If the board won't power up, that's the first thing to check. And never feed the C5 from both the M-Bus 5V and its own USB at once — pick one.

04

The C5-side build

Everything here is wired on the Proto Module between the C5 and its peripherals — all ESP32-C5 GPIO, all 3.3 V. These are the current firmware defaults; the radio and SD share one SPI2 bus, each with its own select.

Wire the 7-pin socket once; the CC1101 and the nRF24 both drop into it. Give G3 a 10k pull-up and G4 a 4.7k pull-down so the bus boots sane.

!

Decouple the PA/LNA. An nRF24+PA/LNA spikes to ~115 mA on TX and will sag the DevKitC's shared LDO. Fit 100 nF + 10 µF + 100 µF at the module's VCC/GND pins and, on the second Proto Module, give it its own 3V3 LDO off the 5 V rail. Full detail in Decouple the nRF24 and the Swappable Radio Header.

05

One module, or two

"One or more" is a real choice: a single module is compact; splitting onto two keeps the hot PA/LNA power stage away from the digital bus and gives you room for the LDO and bulk caps.

Single module

C5 + the 7-pin radio socket + the M-Bus pass-through. Tight, but fine for a bare CC1101 or a decoupled bare nRF24. SD/GPS only if there's room.

Two modules (stacked)

Lower: C5, M-Bus tap, SD, GPS. Upper: the radio socket, its decoupling stack and a dedicated 3V3 LDO off 5 V. Both share the tapped rails through the M-Bus pass-through pins.

i

Stacking shares everything. Modules on the M-Bus pass the same nets straight through. The only lines in play for this build are the four you tapped — the radio/SD bus stays on the C5 and never touches the M-Bus, so a second module just needs the 5 V and GND it carries up.

06

Flash, seat, verify

1

Flash the C5 first, off the module

Flash JanOS over the DevKitC's UART USB-C port (not the native USB port) and prove it on a serial monitor before it goes on the Proto Module. Web flasher at c5lab.github.io/projectZero, or esptool — see the DevKitC build for the exact offsets.

2

Flash the Tab5 app

ESP32-P4 image via c5lab.github.io/M5MonsterC5-Tab5 or esptool (bootloader 0x2000, partitions 0x8000, app 0x10000).

3

Seat on the M-Bus and boot

Mount the module, boot the Tab5. It probes M-Bus/Grove/USB, finds the C5 on UART0, and raises MONSTER DETECTED. A quick ping → pong is the pass/fail.

!

Version & one link. The Tab5 app wants JanOS 1.7.6 or newer (a mismatch is a warning, not a wall). And pick a single link — M-Bus or Grove or the UART USB port; all three land on the same UART0, so wiring two fights on the bus.

07

If something's off

Symptom → check
SymptomLikely cause / fix
Board never powers upM-Bus 5V (pin 28) is the switchable EXT_5V_BUS and may be off — power the C5 from its USB to confirm, or enable the bus.
Tab5 shows no MonsterTX/RX not crossed — C5 G11→M-Bus G38 (pin 13), C5 G12→M-Bus G37 (pin 14); confirm common GND; make sure only one link is wired.
"firmware too old/wrong"C5 is below JanOS 1.7.6 — reflash the C5.
Radio badge NOT DETECTEDCheck 3V3 (not 5V), CSN=G3 (10k↑), control=G4 (4.7k↓), bus G6/G8/G5, and that only one radio is in the socket.
nRF24 drops on TXPA/LNA brownout — add the 100 µF bulk cap and the dedicated 3V3 LDO on the second module.
Reboots when you touch the moduleA stray short to M-Bus EN (pin 6) resets the P4. Keep your wiring clear of pins 6 and the HVIN pins (25/27/29).
