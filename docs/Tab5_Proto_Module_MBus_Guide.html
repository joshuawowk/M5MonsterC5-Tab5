Tab5 Proto Module — the M-Bus

Wire your own circuits to the M5Stack Tab5 through the 2×15 M-Bus connector on its back, using one or more M5Stack Proto Modules. This is the pinout, the power budget, the pins you can actually use, and the ones already spoken for — including the serial link the M5MonsterC5 C5 rides on.

01

What the M-Bus gives you

The M-Bus is M5Stack's stacking backplane — a 2×15 header that fans the host SoC's power rails and a handful of buses and GPIOs out to a module. On the Tab5 it exposes ESP32-P4 pins. An M5Stack Proto Module (a perfboard that seats on the M-Bus) is the simplest way to solder a custom circuit onto it.

Power out

3V3, a switchable 5V bus, battery (BAT) and ground, straight off the connector — no external supply for small loads.

Buses pre-routed

An SPI set, the internal I²C, and UART0 are already broken out — tap them instead of bit-banging.

~9 spare GPIOs

General-purpose P4 pins with nothing assigned — buttons, LEDs, a CS line, an extra UART.

i

Mechanical check first. The Tab5 is a tablet, not a Core-stack body — confirm your Proto Module (or an M5-Bus breakout/extension) physically seats on the Tab5's rear connector before you rely on it. The pinout below is electrical; it does not promise a given module clips on.

03

Power & the rules

The M-Bus hands you three rails, but M5Stack does not publish the Tab5's per-rail current limits. Treat them as "enough for a sensor, not for a motor," and bring your own supply for anything hungry.

  • 3V3 (pin 12) — logic-level rail for small peripherals. No published current budget; keep it to tens of mA and decouple at your board.
  • 5V (pin 28) — the EXT_5V_BUS, gated by EXT5V_EN, so it is not always live. Don't assume 5 V is present; enable it in firmware or power your module another way.
  • BAT (pin 30) — the raw NP-F550 pack, roughly 6.0 V (shutdown) to 8.3 V (full). Unregulated and variable — put your own regulator after it; never feed it into a 3.3 V part.
  • HVIN (pins 25/27/29) — not defined on the M5Stack page. Unknown voltage and direction. Leave unconnected.
  • 3.3 V logic only — ESP32-P4 GPIOs are not 5 V-tolerant. Level-shift anything that swings above 3.3 V before it reaches a pin.
!

Measure before you trust a rail. Current ratings, the HVIN nets, and the connector pitch/pin-1 marking are not stated in the Tab5 documentation text. Put a meter on 3V3/5V/BAT and confirm pin 1 against the board silk or the schematic PDF before committing a build.

04

Proto build recipes

Each recipe uses only free pins or a clearly-labeled shared bus. Solder to the Proto Module's perfboard, run short leads to the M-Bus pads, and decouple every powered part at its own pins.

A. Buttons, LEDs & plain GPIO

The nine free GPIOs (G16, G45, G2, G3, G4, G48, G47, G35, G51) take buttons (to GND, with the P4's internal pull-up) and LEDs (through a resistor to GND). No bus, no config beyond gpio_set_direction.

B. An SPI peripheral

Tie your device to MOSI G18 / MISO G19 / SCK G5 and pick any free GPIO as chip-select (the bus has no CS pin). 3V3 + GND from the connector; decouple at the device.

// ESP-IDF, Tab5 M-Bus SPI + a free-GPIO CS spi_bus_config_t bus = { .mosi_io_num=18, .miso_io_num=19, .sclk_io_num=5 }; spi_device_interface_config_t dev = { .spics_io_num=16 /* G16, free */, .clock_speed_hz=2000000 };

C. An I²C sensor

The internal bus is on SDA G31 / SCL G32. It already carries the Tab5's own devices (BMI270 IMU @0x68, INA226 @0x41, RX8130CE RTC @0x32, audio codecs, IO-expanders), so you may share it — only if your sensor's address doesn't collide. If it might, bit-bang a second I²C on two free GPIOs instead.

D. A second UART

UART0 (G37/G38) is taken by the MonsterC5 C5 link, and the "PC" serial (G6/G7) is best left alone. For your own serial device, assign a spare UART to two free GPIOs — the P4's UART matrix lets you route almost any pin.

E. Stacking more than one module

Every module on the M-Bus shares the same rails and the same SPI/I²C lines. Give each SPI device its own CS GPIO and each I²C device a unique address, and add the modules' current draw together against the (unpublished, so measured) rail budget.

05

Conflicts to design around

✕

The C5 link lives on the M-Bus. The M5MonsterC5 firmware's default serial port to the ESP32-C5 is M-Bus UART0 — TXD0 G37 / RXD0 G38 (pins 13/14). If the Monster C5 is connected over M-Bus, those two pins are gone for proto use. Either move the C5 to the Grove port (the app auto-detects it there) and keep the M-Bus for your circuit, or keep the C5 on M-Bus and avoid pins 13/14.

  • Internal I²C is shared — G31/G32 run the Tab5's own sensors. Don't hold the bus, and never reuse an address already on it.
  • EN / RST (pin 6) resets the P4. A stray pull here reboots the tablet.
  • PC_RX/PC_TX (G6/G7) look like the programming/console path — driving them can break flashing or the console.
  • HVIN (25/27/29) is undocumented. Unknown until the schematic says otherwise — leave it open.
  • P4 pins are 3.3 V — no 5 V on a GPIO, ever.
06

Reference & what's unconfirmed

The pinout is transcribed from the official M5Stack Tab5 page (PinMap → M-Bus). A few facts a careful build needs are not in that page's text and should be confirmed on the hardware or the schematic.

Confirmed (M5Stack page)

All 30 pin assignments and GPIO numbers; UART0 = G37/G38; SPI = G18/G19/G5; internal I²C = G31/G32; 5V is the switchable EXT_5V_BUS; BAT is the NP-F550 pack.

Not confirmed — verify

Per-rail current limits (3V3 / 5V / BAT); what HVIN is and its voltage; connector pitch and pin-1 marking; whether M-Bus G4 and the panel's "PI4IO G4" are the same net (they are not — one is a P4 GPIO, the other an IO-expander pin).
