Build the MonsterC5 on a Proto Module — XIAO C5

The proto build, re-cut for a Seeed XIAO ESP32-C5 and a modified JanOS that no longer hard-codes one shared chip-select. Each radio gets its own CS + control pin, so the E01-2G4M27SX (nRF24+PA/LNA) and the E07-433M20S (CC1101+PA) both stay soldered down at once — no DPDT switch. The build spans two stacked proto boards that share the M-Bus pins, and every wire is drawn to scale, pad to pad.

The short version. On the XIAO the stock C5 radio pins aren't reachable (they're the battery pad and the underside JTAG pads), so this build runs a re-pinned JanOS anyway — and while we're re-pinning, we give the CC1101 and the nRF24 separate chip-select and control lines instead of the one pair stock firmware shared. Both radios hang on the same SPI2 bus (SCK/MOSI/MISO) permanently; each answers only to its own CS. No select switch, no swapping — you still run one radio app at a time, that's all. It takes two proto boards: the XIAO on one, the radios and their regulator on the other, joined by the bus pins they share when stacked.

01

What changes on the XIAO — and why it needs the re-pin

This is the same electrical build as the DevKitC proto guide, with two differences that come straight from the hardware: the XIAO's pad map, and a firmware change that de-shares the chip-select.

reason 1

The stock pins aren't on the XIAO

Stock JanOS uses SCK=G6, MISO=G5, CS=G3, GDO0/CE=G4. On the XIAO G6 is the battery-sense pad and G5/G3/G4 are JTAG pads on the underside. So a re-pin to the XIAO's broken-out pads is mandatory — Wi-Fi works stock, but no radio will.

reason 2

Dedicated CS = both radios live

Since we're re-pinning anyway, give each radio its own CS + control. The CC1101 lands on D4/D5, the nRF24 on D0/D1. Now both sit on the shared bus permanently; the deselected one tri-states MISO. The DPDT switch the other guide needs is gone.

unchanged

The hard parts still bite

The E07 is a +PA part — TX_EN/RX_EN steer its RF switch (Power & PA). The E01 pulls ~490 mA on TX — it needs its own 3V3 LDO + bulk cap. And both Ebyte parts are 1.27 mm SMD: they need adapter boards.

Two pin domains — keep them straight

host side

The M-Bus tap — 4 wires

All the board takes from the Tab5: 5V, GND, and the UART0 pair G37/G38 (ESP32-P4 pins). The XIAO's console is on D6/D7 (= G11/G12) — it lines up with the tap unchanged.

board side

The XIAO build — its own pads

The radio nets leave the XIAO's own pads (D0/D1/D4/D5/D8/D9/D10) and cross to the radio board on seven M-Bus pins the Tab5 leaves idle. With the two console pads that's nine of the eleven used; D2 and D3 stay free — no SD hangs off the C5 (storage is the Tab5's internal microSD).

The change that makes both radios coexist is in the CC1101 and nRF24 rows: the CC1101 keeps a dedicated pair on D4/D5, and the nRF24 moves to its own D0/D1 — they no longer collide on G3/G4. These are the exact edits in the branch:

// components/cc1101/include/cc1101.h — dedicated CC1101 pins #define CC1101_PIN_SCK 8 // D8 #define CC1101_PIN_MISO 9 // D9 #define CC1101_PIN_MOSI 10 // D10 #define CC1101_PIN_CS 23 // D4 — own chip-select #define CC1101_PIN_GDO0 24 // D5 — own control // components/nrf24_jammer/nrf24_jammer.c — dedicated nRF24 pins #define NRF24_CS_PIN 1 // D0 — own chip-select (no longer G3) #define NRF24_CE_PIN 0 // D1 — own control (no longer G4)
i

Boot-safe pads. ESP32-C5 strapping pins are G2/G3/G7/G25/G26/G27/G28. None of the pads this build wires lands on one: D8/D9/D10 are the XIAO's native, non-strapping SPI pads, and the radios' CS/ctrl sit on D0/D1/D4/D5 (G1/G0/G23/G24). The two free pads, D2 (G25) and D3 (G7), are both strapping pins — fine, since nothing in normal operation drives them (D3 is the firmware's transient SPI self-test scratch pad).

One board is too tight for a XIAO, an 18×32 mm E07, an E01 and a regulator, so this build uses two PROTO-2.54 boards on one M-Bus stack: Board A (the board inside the Proto Module) carries the XIAO; Board B (an added Proto Board) carries both radios and their 3V3 regulator. Shared pins reach the Tab5 too. Seven of these nets ride on bus pins that are also ESP32-P4 GPIOs (G16/G17/G45/G52/G6/G48/G51). The MonsterC5 Tab5 app never configures them — it only uses G37/G38 on the bus — so they float and the lines are yours. Don't launch another Tab5 app that drives those pins while this stack is attached.

Never borrow: pins 17/18 (the Tab5's live internal I²C, G31/G32), 24 (G35, boot strap), 25/27/29 (HVIN — the tablet's high-voltage DC input rail), 30 (BAT), 6 (EN). And don't tie the regulator's output to pin 12 — that's the Tab5's own 3V3 rail.

05

Stacking & assembly

The wiring diagram above is the layout — parts sit where they're drawn. These are the things the drawing can't show: how the boards stack, how a 1.27 mm module goes onto a 2.54 mm board, and how to read the pad numbers.

Stack order

Tab5 → Board A (Proto Module) → Board B (Proto Board). Electrically the order doesn't matter — every bus pin is common to the whole stack — but putting the radios outermost keeps them away from the tablet and lets the IPX pigtails exit freely.

Mounting the two SMD modules

1.27 mm castellations don't land on 2.54 mm holes. Insulate under each module (Kapton or foam tape — the proto pads are bare copper and the modules have pads underneath), stick it down shield-up, then run 30 AWG from each castellation. A 1.27→2.54 adapter also works; then wire by pad number, not position.

Reading the pad numbers

Shield up, pad edge at the top, pads count right-to-left: E07 1…12, E01 1…8. Flip the module and they count left-to-right. In the diagram the pad edge faces right, so pad 1 is the bottom pad on both modules.

Bus tap pads

Each M-Bus pin has a small solder pad beside the connector. Even pins are the inner column, next to the hole grid. Odd pins (GND, pin 13) are the outer column at the board edge — run round the end of the connector or use the labelled pads on the back.

XIAO on the grid

Pad rows are 15.24 mm apart — exactly 6 holes. USB-C up: left column D0…D6, right column 5V, GND, 3V3, D10, D9, D8, D7. Drawn in hole columns 6 and 12, rows 2–8, with the USB-C at the board edge.

Check the height

The gap between stacked boards is only a few millimetres in a classic Proto Module. A XIAO soldered flat is ≈4.5 mm; the radios are 2.4–3.0 mm. Lay the 470 µF cans on their side or use low-profile parts — or use the taller Proto 13.2 frame.

No SD on the C5 — and that frees up pads. The MonsterC5 stores to the Tab5's internal microSD; nothing SD-related wires to the C5, so D2 and D3 stay free. If you want on-board GPS, there's room — but the stock GPS pins G13/G14 aren't broken out on the XIAO, so remap the GPS UART onto those two pads in firmware. GPS and E07 transmit (which also wants two pads) won't both fit — for both, use the DevKitC build.

06

Power & the PA switch

Both modules are +PA parts and both bite back if you skimp on the supply or feed them over 3.6 V. This is where builds that "almost work" fall down.

Give the radios their own 3V3

The E01 draws ~490 mA at 27 dBm in millisecond bursts (RX ~22 mA). The XIAO's regulator — and its 3V3 pad — won't hold 3V3 under that. Run an LDO ≥ 600 mA from M-Bus 5V, and put 470 µF + 0.1 µF right at each radio's VCC/GND. Star the grounds at the LDO.

3.3 V is the ceiling

Over 3.6 V permanently damages both modules. They are not 5 V-tolerant on any pin — power or signal. The XIAO is a 3.3 V part, so its pads are fine; just never let the M-Bus 5V touch a module pin.

Driving the E07's RF switch (TX_EN / RX_EN)

The E07-433M20S puts a PA and an antenna switch in front of the CC1101. Two pins steer it, and they're mutually exclusive:

  • Receive / scan only (what stock firmware does): tie RX_EN → 3V3 and TX_EN → GND. The module sits in receive; the CC1101 talks over SPI as normal. No firmware change.
  • Transmit too: wire TX_EN and RX_EN to two spare pads and drive TX_EN high (RX_EN low) only while sending, low again after. Never both high. The XIAO's two free pads (D2/D3) cover this — enough for the TX_EN/RX_EN pair or an optional GPS UART, but not both at once.
!

The bare-CC1101 firmware never touches TX_EN/RX_EN. If you leave them floating the PA switch is undefined and range collapses — always tie them, even for receive. The plain E07-433M10S (10-pin, no PA) has no such pins; this applies only to the M20S you ordered.

07

Flash & verify

1

Build the dual-CS JanOS and flash the XIAO off-board

Socket the XIAO so you can pull it. Check out the branch with the XIAO pin map (section 03), build for the C5 target, and flash over the XIAO's USB-C.

# ESP32-C5 → IDF 6.1 source ~/.espressif/v6.1/esp-idf/export.sh git checkout feat/xiao-c5-dedicated-radio-pins idf.py -C JanOS/ESP32C5 set-target esp32c5 build flash
2

Stack the boards & power from the Tab5

Plug Board B onto Board A, then the pair onto the Tab5's M-Bus. M-Bus 5V (pin 28) is switchable — if the board is dark, enable the bus or power the XIAO from USB to prove the build first.

3

Confirm the link on the Tab5

The Tab5 app talks UART0 at 115200 8N1 and expects JanOS 1.7.6+ (a mismatch warns, it doesn't block). A Monster appears when TX/RX are crossed (XIAO D6→M-Bus G38, D7→G37) and GND is common.

4

Prove each radio on its own CS

No switch to flip: open the CC1101 app and run its self-test (answers on D4), then stop it and open the nRF24 app (answers on D0). A good SPI read-back on each means the shared bus and that radio's CSN/CE are right. Both are wired the whole time; only one app runs at once.
