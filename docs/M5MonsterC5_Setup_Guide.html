M5MonsterC5 Setup Guide

Build the dual-band Wi-Fi + sub-GHz + 2.4 GHz add-on for the M5Stack Tab5 — wire the ESP32-C5 and a radio on its swappable header (CC1101 for sub-GHz or an nRF24L01+PA/LNA for 2.4 GHz), flash both halves of the firmware, and confirm it live. Works with the store-bought M5MonsterC5 board or a DIY ESP32-C5 dev board.

01

How the stack fits together

Three parts, one wire harness. The Tab5 is only a screen and keyboard; every radio operation runs on the ESP32-C5 (firmware: JanOS). The C5 drives a single swappable radio header — fit a CC1101 for sub-GHz, or an nRF24L01+PA/LNA for 2.4 GHz. They talk to the Tab5 over a plain serial link.

i

Buy or build? The $75 M5MonsterC5 board is the ESP32-C5 pre-mounted with the Grove/M5Bus connectors, an SD slot and a GPS port — plug-and-play. A DIY ESP32-C5-DevKitC-1 (~$15) does the same electrically; you add the radio and (optionally) a microSD breakout with a few jumpers. This guide covers both — the wiring below is identical, the store board just pre-makes the Tab5 connection.

02

What you need

Minimum is an ESP32-C5 and the Tab5. Add a radio for the header — a CC1101 for sub-GHz, an nRF24L01+PA/LNA for 2.4 GHz — plus an SD card for capture storage and captive portals, and GPS for wardriving.

required

ESP32-C5 board

M5MonsterC5, or an ESP32-C5-DevKitC-1. Must be the C5 — it is the only ESP32 with 2.4 and 5 GHz Wi-Fi.

required

M5Stack Tab5

ESP32-P4 tablet. Runs the M5MonsterC5-Tab5 UI app.

sub-GHz

CC1101 module

Any 3.3 V CC1101 breakout — E07-M1101D, or the SMD E07-433M20S (+20 dBm). Needs SCK/MOSI/MISO/CSN/GDO0 broken out.

2.4 GHz

nRF24L01+PA/LNA

A +PA/LNA module such as the Ebyte E01-2G4M27SX (nRF24L01P, +27 dBm). 3.3 V only, and it must be decoupled — see the wiring section.

storage

microSD + breakout

On the store board it's built in. For DIY, a 3.3 V SPI microSD breakout on the shared bus.

optional

GPS module

ATGM336H (9600) or M5Stack GPS v1.1 (115200) for wardrive location.

i

LoRa is not sub-GHz here. A LoRa module like the E22-900M22S (SX1262) is a different chip from the CC1101 — JanOS does not drive it, so it won't appear on the SubGHz or nRF24 screens. Noted only because it's easy to buy by mistake alongside the E07; treat SX1262/LoRa as future work, not part of this build.

03

Wiring

Two harnesses: a 4-wire UART from the Tab5 to the C5, and one 3.3 V radio on the C5's SPI2 bus (the same bus the SD card uses — each device just gets its own chip-select). The CC1101 and the nRF24 land on the same swappable header: identical SCK/MOSI/MISO, and they share the chip-select (G3) and control pin (G4). Fit one radio at a time. All logic is 3.3 V.

Change these defaults, if you must, in components/cc1101/include/cc1101.h (CC1101_PIN_SCK / MISO / MOSI / CS / GDO0). MOSI and MISO were moved off GPIO7/GPIO2 — both are C5 strapping pins and RC-loaded on the DevKitC, so SPI never clocked reliably there.

Pins live in components/nrf24_jammer/nrf24_jammer.c (NRF24_SCK/MOSI/MISO/CS/CE_PIN). On the Ebyte E01-2G4M27SX, CE also switches the LNA — the firmware drives it.

!

Decouple the PA/LNA, or it browns out on TX. A +PA/LNA module spikes to ~115 mA on transmit. Fit a 100 nF + 10 µF + 100 µF stack across VCC–GND at the module's own pins (smallest closest), and feed VCC from a stiff 3.3 V — for the PA/LNA, ideally a dedicated 3V3 LDO off 5 V, never a GPIO and never 5 V direct. Full build, with a 3D view and a bill of materials, is in the companion guides: Decouple the nRF24, the Swappable Radio Header, and the 5× Penta nRF24 Daughterboard.

✕

One radio at a time, and mind the reserved pins. The CC1101 and nRF24 share G3 (CSN) and G4 (GDO0 / CE) — never socket both at once. GPIO15–22 are the C5's in-package flash + Quad-PSRAM (MSPI) bus — touching them bricks the boot. GPIO11/12 are the console UART the Tab5 depends on. GPIO2 and GPIO7 are strapping pins (why MOSI/MISO moved to G8/G5). The SD card rides the same SPI2 bus on its own CS G10; GPS is its own UART on G13/G14.

SD shares the same three SPI2 lines as the radio header (MISO G5 / MOSI G8 / CLK G6) with its own CS (GPIO10) — both live on the bus at once. GPS is its own UART.

04

Flash the firmware

Two independent images: JanOS on the ESP32-C5 and the M5MonsterC5-Tab5 app on the Tab5. Do the C5 first so it's ready when the Tab5 comes up. The web flashers need Chrome/Edge (WebSerial).

1

Flash JanOS onto the ESP32-C5

Easiest — the web flasher. Plug the C5 into USB, open the flasher, pick the port, flash.

# web flasher (Chrome/Edge) → https://c5lab.github.io/projectZero/janos_flash.html

Or build this SubGHz-enabled tree from source with ESP-IDF v5.4.x and flash over USB:

# from JanOS/ESP32C5 $ . $IDF_PATH/export.sh $ idf.py set-target esp32c5 # already set $ idf.py build $ idf.py -p /dev/ttyACM0 flash monitor

Raw esptool offsets, if you prefer them (from the build output):

$ python -m esptool --chip esp32c5 -b 460800 write_flash \ --flash_mode dio --flash_size 8MB --flash_freq 80m \ 0x2000 build/bootloader/bootloader.bin \ 0x8000 build/partition_table/partition-table.bin \ 0xf000 build/ota_data_initial.bin \ 0x20000 build/projectZero.bin
2

Flash the app onto the Tab5

Same idea on the ESP32-P4 side.

# web flasher (Chrome/Edge) → https://c5lab.github.io/M5MonsterC5-Tab5/janos_flash.html

Or with esptool (ESP32-P4): bootloader 0x2000, partition-table 0x8000, app 0x10000.

$ python -m esptool --chip esp32p4 -b 460800 write_flash \ 0x2000 bootloader.bin \ 0x8000 partition-table.bin \ 0x10000 M5MonsterC5-Tab5.bin
3

Power up together

With the harness connected, boot the Tab5. It probes the M5Bus/Grove/USB port, finds the C5, and reads its firmware version.

!

Version lock. The Tab5 app requires JanOS 1.7.6 or newer (JANOS_VERSION_REQUIRED — the check is a minimum, not an exact match). An older C5 shows a "firmware too old/wrong" popup and disables features. The tree in this repo already reports 1.7.6 — if you flashed an older release, update JanOS.

06

Bench-tuning & coverage

The firmware compiles clean and speaks the protocol exactly, but the RF constants ship as sensible defaults — they were not tuned against a live radio. Budget one bench session.

  • RF timing constants — RX bandwidth, data rate, glitch/idle thresholds and the TX inter-frame gap live in cc1101.c / subghz_capture.c. Adjust against a known remote.
  • Decoders — Princeton / PT2262 / EV1527 fixed-code remotes decode to serial + button. Anything else is captured as Unknown but still saves and replays (Flipper RAW-style).
  • Weather — the Nexus-TH family decodes; the framework in subghz_decode.c is built to add Acurite / LaCrosse / etc.
  • Rolling codes — KeeLoq and friends cannot be cloned; they're reported, not replayed.
  • nRF24 bus — if the DevKitC's MISO line is RC-slow, the driver falls back to a bit-banged SPI read; detection still works but keep leads short.
!

Transmit responsibly. Jammer and replay actively transmit. Know your local rules, use only on hardware you own, and keep power low on the bench.
